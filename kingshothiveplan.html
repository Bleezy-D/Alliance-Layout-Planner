3 · The trap-packing debate, settled: flush swirl beats banner-padding — but by seconds

Both camps simulated exhaustively: 2×2 TCs packed around a 3×3 trap, (left) padded to 4×4 with 7 banners so TCs tile evenly, vs (right) TCs pushed flush with each side offset one tile from the next — the swirl. Every legal placement was enumerated and cities ranked by center-to-center march distance. The swirl's 8 flush cities sit at 2.55–2.92 tiles while banner-padding’s 8th-closest city is at 3.81; within 5 tiles the swirl fits 20 TCs vs 16, within 7 tiles 36 vs 35. Rank by rank it is equal or better at 104 of the first 110 positions, and never worse by more than 0.23 tiles. The padded block can’t win: its banners physically occupy seven flush tiles that swirl gives to real cities, and banners next to a trap confer no buff. But keep the magnitude honest — the worst-case gap is 0.89 tiles, roughly 2–3 seconds one-way, 5–6 round-trip. Over a 30-minute Bear Hunt with 60–120s rally cycles that is at most one extra rally for a handful of members. Swirl wins; nobody should switch servers over it.
